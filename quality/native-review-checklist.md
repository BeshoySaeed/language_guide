# German native-review protocol

This protocol is the mandatory human gate for the German curriculum. Automated validation cannot approve language quality.

## Reviewer eligibility

The reviewer must:

- be a native German speaker and record their real name and locale;
- be able to judge contemporary standard German and common everyday register;
- review the complete level, not a sample;
- have no unresolved authorship conflict that prevents an independent decision.

## Full-level review

For every chapter and lesson, check:

- German is idiomatic, current, natural, and appropriate for the stated CEFR level;
- English translations preserve the intended meaning and register;
- articles, gender, plurals, cases, conjugation, tense, and word order are correct;
- vocabulary load and grammar progression are appropriate at that point in the course;
- dialogues sound natural for the setting and formal or informal address is consistent;
- examples teach reusable language rather than awkward translations;
- pronunciation metadata and speech playback contain only the intended German utterance;
- each question has one unambiguously correct answer and a useful explanation;
- reading, listening, writing, and speaking prompts are answerable from the material;
- cultural and practical claims are accurate and not misleading.

## A1 required scope

The report must record a decision for all 14 A1 chapters:

1. Sound and structure
2. Everyday essentials
3. My life and my day
4. Out and about
5. Wellbeing and plans
6. Calendar and identity
7. Work and study
8. Home and neighborhood
9. Food and shopping
10. Travel basics
11. Communication and safety
12. Grammar consolidation
13. A1 bridge
14. A1 review and production

The reviewer must also complete the A1 seven-skill final in desktop and mobile layouts and listen to at least one vocabulary item and one useful phrase in every chapter.

## Finding severity

- **Critical:** teaches materially wrong German, exposes unsafe advice, loses learner data, or blocks the course/final.
- **High:** repeated unnatural or incorrect language, an invalid assessment answer, broken speech language/rate, or a major accessibility barrier.
- **Medium:** localized wording, sequencing, explanation, or usability problem that does not block learning.
- **Low:** polish, preference, or optional enhancement.

Every finding needs an anonymous ID, level, chapter, lesson or screen, severity, evidence, expected correction, status, and verification note.

## Approval rule

Approval is allowed only when every chapter and final were reviewed, all critical and high findings were closed and rechecked, no systematic error remains, and the reviewer signs and dates the report.

Start from "quality/a1-native-review-report.example.json". Only after genuine approval may the book provenance receive a nativeReview object.
